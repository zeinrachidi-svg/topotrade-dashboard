Covering the most recently completed US session, Thursday, September 10, 2026 — the fourth straight day of declines as US oil prices pushed above $100 a barrel on fears that a prolonged Middle East conflict could stoke inflation. The S&P 500 fell 0.58% to 7,591.70 and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.65% to 26,081.72.

SpaceX (SPCX) traded in a $144.89–$154.70 range and closed around $148.76, little changed from the prior close of $147.55. No major company-specific news that session; the stock has traded as a listed name since its June 12, 2026 IPO.

Intel (INTC) pulled back sharply, down roughly 5–6% intraday on profit-taking after a parabolic run — still up about 188% year to date and 18% in the past week alone on reports it may raise PC processor prices. Piper Sandler initiated coverage at Neutral, suggesting investors wait for a better entry point.

IBM traded narrowly around $239, following a roughly 1.2% drop in the prior session as oil prices weighed on the broader market. IBM announced an open-source AI model with NASA for lunar observation data, and a new quantum computing hub with Lockheed Martin at ETH Zurich.

Vanguard S&P 500 UCITS ETF (VUAA) moved in line with the S&P 500's decline — no company-specific news, as expected for an index tracker.

NVIDIA (NVDA) fell about 2% to around $219. The company formally announced a $12.9 billion acquisition of Hugging Face, and CEO Jensen Huang spoke at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference. Nvidia is also facing a DOJ probe over its licensing deal with AI startup Groq. The stock remains up about 21% year to date.

Moderna (MRNA) was little changed near $135. The company lowered 2026 operating expense guidance by $200M and cut its cash-cost outlook by $200M while reaffirming up to 10% revenue growth for the year; sentiment has also been helped by promising early results from its personalized mRNA cancer vaccine.

ASML traded little changed around €1,498. The company continues to deepen its High-NA EUV lithography partnership with Samsung, and broke ground on a second major campus in the Netherlands' Brainport region amid rising AI-driven demand for chipmaking equipment.
