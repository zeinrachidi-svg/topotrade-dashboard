Portfolio

Covering the last completed US session, Monday, September 14, 2026.

SpaceX (SPCX)

Shares slid about 2% in early trading to $148 amid the broad AI-stock selloff and continuing post-IPO insider lockup releases; Greenlight Capital's David Einhorn warned the $1.75 trillion IPO valuation could mark a speculative market top. The stock recovered through the session to reclaim the $150 level as SpaceX's market cap neared $2 trillion, with some Wall Street targets pointing toward $200. Separately, SpaceX launched three satellites for SES on a Falcon 9 on Sunday — its 700th Falcon launch to date.

Intel (INTC)

Shares fell about 5.6% to close near $97, part of the broad semiconductor selloff tied to AI-safety concerns. Apple also told Mac App Store developers it may drop support for Intel-based Macs in apps requiring macOS 13 or later, another step away from Intel silicon. The stock remains up sharply for the year, +163% year-to-date.

IBM (IBM)

Bucked the sector's decline, rising about 2.4% to close near $247 after Bank of America lifted its price target from $315 to $330. IBM continues to lean on AI and quantum-computing partnerships, including with NASA and Lockheed Martin.

Vanguard S&P 500 Acc UCITS ETF (VUAA)

Tracks the S&P 500, which closed down 0.48% at 7,619.95 as the broader market retreated — see the market note below.

NVIDIA (NVDA)

Shares fell about 3.4% to close near $218, caught up in the AI-stock selloff after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay urging a slowdown in frontier AI development, a call echoed by OpenAI's Sam Altman and Elon Musk over the weekend. Nvidia's $12.9 billion Hugging Face acquisition, announced September 3, and a possible investment in Anthropic's IPO remain in focus longer term.

Moderna (MRNA)

One of the day's bright spots — shares closed around $147, supported by two clinical wins: the Merck-partnered personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine hit its Phase 3 goals, and the seasonal flu shot mFLUSIVA received FDA approval. The company also trimmed its 2026 expense guidance while reaffirming up to 10% revenue growth.

ASML Holding (ASML)

Shares fell sharply, about 7.2%, to close near $1,575, underperforming the broader market on the same AI-slowdown worries plus lingering export-control and geopolitical concerns. Analysts stayed constructive — Cantor Fitzgerald reiterated an Overweight rating with a €2,500 target.

Broader market

Major indexes fell Monday as investors weighed the AI-slowdown call from Amodei and Altman, a resignation from an Anthropic researcher warning of safety risks, and a spike in oil prices (Brent above $107) tied to escalating Middle East tensions, including attacks on Saudi shipping and energy infrastructure. The S&P 500 closed down 0.48% at 7,619.95, the Dow slipped 0.29% to 52,421.28, and the Nasdaq lost 0.56% to 26,186.41. Treasury yields rose to multi-year highs — the 10-year touched 5%, its highest since 2023 — as markets priced in roughly an 85% chance of a Fed rate hike this week. Semiconductor and hardware names led the losses while software and cybersecurity stocks, including CrowdStrike and Palo Alto Networks, outperformed.
