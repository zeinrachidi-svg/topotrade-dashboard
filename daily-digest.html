5. Portfolio

SpaceX (SPCX) rallied on Thursday, up about 2.6% to close near $154.81 after swinging between a low of $144.36 and a rebound driven by Starship-launch optimism and speculation Elon Musk raised about a possible SpaceX–Tesla combination. The stock remains well below its post-IPO high but is one of the more volatile names in the portfolio this week.

Intel (INTC) jumped about 7.7% to close at $108.80 on reports of possible SK Hynix talks over U.S. memory manufacturing at an Intel Ohio fab (SK Hynix said nothing is finalized), plus news of a planned ~10% PC CPU price increase in October and progress on High-NA EUV volume manufacturing. Several analysts raised price targets on the back of the news.

IBM was little changed to slightly lower, last around $237.49, after earlier weakness tied to a trimmed 2026 revenue growth outlook (cut to 4–5%) following a Q2 miss, partly offset by a $1 billion CHIPS Act award supporting its quantum and semiconductor work.

Vanguard S&P 500 UCITS ETF (VUAA) moved with the broader market — up on the day as the S&P 500 rallied following the Fed's rate move.

NVIDIA (NVDA) rose to around $219.34 from a $213.90 prior close, supported by continued bullish commentary from Jensen Huang on chip demand potentially doubling next year and NVIDIA's push toward a "neocloud" partner model with regional cloud providers.

Moderna (MRNA) jumped about 7% to roughly $154.49–$155.56 after presenting at the Morgan Stanley Global Healthcare Conference, where investors responded positively to progress on its personalized cancer vaccine (intismeran) and recent respiratory-portfolio approvals. The stock is up sharply for the year but still below its August 52-week high.

ASML (ASML/ASME) gained to around €1,423.80 from a €1,396.20 prior close, with JPMorgan reiterating a Buy rating and a new collaboration announced with Xanadu Quantum on lithography for photonic quantum hardware.

Broader market: the S&P 500 rallied about 1.1% and the Nasdaq Composite jumped about 1.7% on Thursday, a day after the Fed raised its benchmark rate a quarter point to 3.75–4% — its first hike in three years. Fed officials' projections point to another possible hike later this year, which kept sentiment constructive into the close.
